# Purchasing — user guide

One website, one sign-in. What you see depends on what you do.

Sign in at the site address with your company email and password. Forgot it? **Forgot your
password** on the sign-in screen, then ask the office — the system does not send mail yet, so an
administrator hands over a reset.

---

## Foremen and field workers — "My requests"

### Raise a request (works on a phone)

**New request** →

- **Job number** — the job this material is for. One request covers one job.
- **Need-by date and time** — when you need it on site. There is no "priority" field; a date and
  a time say more than the word "urgent".
- **Deliver or pick up**, and where.
- **Items** — what you need, how many, and the unit. Add as many lines as you like.
- **Why** — one line. It is what the workshop reads first.

Submit. It goes straight to the workshop queue.

You are never asked for a vendor, a price, or what is in the workshop. Those are the workshop's
decisions, and the form will not accept them.

### Follow it

**My requests** lists everything open. The status says where it is: *Pending workshop review*,
*Approved*, *Ordered*, *Partially received*, *Received*, *Completed*.

If the workshop asks a question, the request shows **Clarification requested** — open it, answer,
and it goes straight back to the queue. Only you can answer your own clarification.

### Confirm a delivery — "Deliveries"

If you are a foreman assigned to a job site, **Deliveries** lists what is on its way there.
Open one when the truck arrives and record what actually came:

- quantity received (partial is normal and expected)
- damaged, backordered, or short
- notes, and the date it arrived

You see only your own job sites. You cannot confirm another foreman's delivery, confirm a
workshop delivery, or change anything about the order — quantity, vendor, or price.

Receiving less than was ordered is fine; the request stays open until every line is accounted
for. Receiving **more** than was ordered needs a written reason.

## Workshop — Mike and Rick

**Workshop queue**, grouped by what has to happen next: to review, waiting on the field, needs a
PO, needs sending, open orders, received. Each tab shows a count; overdue rows are marked.

### Review a request

- **Section A** is what the field submitted. It is read-only, permanently.
- **Section B** is yours: how many are usable on the shelf, how many the job needs, what to
  order, from whom, and the estimated cost. The suggestion is *approved minus stock* and never
  goes below zero; type over it to buy extra for the shelf, and say why in one line.
- **Section C**: approve, reject (with a reason), or send it back with a question.

Nothing you type here changes Section A.

### After approval

**Generate purchase order** — issues a permanent number and a PDF.
**Create vendor email draft** — straight from the purchase order page. Read it, edit it, mark it
reviewed (after which the words freeze), approve it to send, open it in your own mail client,
then mark it sent. *This system does not send mail. A person does.*

Then **mark ordered**, add tracking, and receive against it as material arrives — at the shop
counter or via the foreman on site.

---

## Office

**Office** shows every active order: what is late, what is partly delivered, what is waiting on
a PO. You can add tracking, record receiving, add notes and open any request's full history and
evidence. You cannot approve a purchase unless an administrator has granted you that authority
specifically.

## Accounting

**Accounting** shows what arrived, with the evidence: PO number, job, vendor, who signed, when,
quantities, and anything that did not match — short, damaged or backordered. Read-only by
design; the role carries no write permission.

---

## Things worth knowing

- **Nothing is emailed automatically.** Every vendor message is a draft a human reviews and
  sends by hand.
- **The history is permanent.** Every meaningful action is recorded with who did it and when,
  including refusals.
- **Sessions expire** after twelve hours. You are told that is what happened, not just logged
  out.
- **If a page says the area is not available to you**, that is not a bug — your account does not
  carry that permission. An administrator can change it.
