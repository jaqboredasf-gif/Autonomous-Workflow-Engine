# What the prize unlocks

**A living model, deliberately not finalised.** The right answer in April 2027 depends on facts that
do not exist in September 2026 — chiefly whether there is a paying customer by then — and an
allocation written now would be a guess with a spreadsheet around it.

**First: the prize is not fixed either.** $10,000 for first place is two editions old. The pool has
changed shape in four of the last five editions, and the 10th annual structure is UNKNOWN. See
`competition-intelligence.md` §2b.

---

## The rule

> **The prize should accelerate a company that is already moving. It should not start anything.**

Any allocation that describes work which would not otherwise happen is describing a company that
was waiting for a prize, and a judge hears that clearly. The strongest answer names something
already in motion and says how much sooner the money makes it happen.

**Banned answers:** "marketing and development", "hiring", "scaling", "growth". They are what
everybody says, they are unfalsifiable, and none of them is a decision.

---

## Candidate uses, and what each actually buys

Costs are order-of-magnitude and must be replaced with real quotes before any of this is said aloud.
An unresearched number in front of a judge who has bought that thing is worse than no number.

| Use | Buys | Estimated | Only worth it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Customer deployment infrastructure** | Hosting, backups and monitoring for external installations, so a second customer is not run from a laptop | low | there is a second customer, or one is committed |
| **Security and compliance work** | An external review of tenant isolation and authority enforcement — a real blocker for any customer with a finance director | medium | a customer has asked, or is about to |
| **Specific integrations** | The one integration that removes double entry — the accounting system the first customer actually uses | medium | discovery says the same integration blocks more than one prospect |
| **Customer discovery** | Travel and time to sit with contractors in their offices | low | it is happening anyway, which today it is not |
| **Paid external pilots** | Paying a business for its time to run a pilot properly | medium-high | a design partner exists and time is what they lack |
| **Legal** | Entity, contracts, a data-processing agreement a real company will sign | low-medium | a customer is about to pay |
| **The founder's time** | Runway to work on this rather than on something else | — | *see below* |

**On buying the founder's time:** it is the honest answer for many student ventures and it is the
weakest one to say out loud, because it is unmeasurable and it sounds like a stipend. If it is the
real answer, say it plainly and attach it to a specific deliverable and date.

## The allocation is a function of the gate

`programs/venture/gates.mjs` already tracks which gate the company is at. The prize arrives in
late April 2027, and the right allocation depends entirely on which gate is open then.

| Gate at the time | Where the money should go | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Production ready** (open today) | Nothing. The blockers are hours, not money. | Spending money on a company that has not yet installed its product is spending it on the wrong problem. Say so; it is a better answer than a plan. |
| **2 — First real proof** | Deployment infrastructure and the founder's time | The bottleneck is running long enough to measure something |
| **3 — Repeatable** | External deployment infrastructure, security review | The bottleneck is being installable somewhere else without hand-holding |
| **4 — Commercial** | Discovery travel, a paid pilot, legal | The bottleneck is people, not code |
| **5 — IIC ready** | Integrations, and the first thing a paying customer asked for | The bottleneck is being worth paying more for |

**Today the honest answer is the first row, and it is a strong answer:**

> Right now money is not the constraint — the next three things this needs are a morning with a
> stopwatch, five phone calls, and a signature. If that is still true in April, I would say so. If
> it is not, it will be because there is a customer, and then the money goes to making the second
> installation something they never have to think about.

That answer demonstrates judgement, which is what the question is actually testing. "Marketing and
development" demonstrates the opposite.

## What to prepare before April

1. **Replace every estimate above with a real quote.** One afternoon.
2. **Re-read the gate** the week of the final and pick the row. It is one line to say.
3. **Have one specific, costed thing ready** — a named integration with a named customer waiting for
   it beats any category of spending.
4. **Check the actual prize structure** for the 10th annual. Planning around $10,000 when the split
   has changed is an avoidable error.
