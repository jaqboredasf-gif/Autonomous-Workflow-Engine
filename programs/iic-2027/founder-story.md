# The founder story

**It answers two questions and no others:**

> **Why did you see this problem before somebody else?**
> **Why will you keep building it?**

Everything else — the degree, the timeline, the ambitions — belongs to a different document.
There is **no team criterion in the published judging rubric**, so this story is not scored on its
own. It earns its place by making the *problem* credible, and it costs a beat only because a
problem described by somebody who lived it is believed and a problem described by a researcher is
not.

**In the four-minute pitch this is one clause, not a beat.** Something with the shape of *"I was the
one carrying the paper"*, said inside the opening, and then never returned to.

---

## What the repository can actually attest

These are facts a person outside the project can check, which is the only kind worth using:

| Fact | Where it is recorded |
|---|---|
| The business is a real, named electrical contractor | `docs/planning/CONTEXT.md`, `PROJECT_SCOPE.md` |
| The founder interviewed the person who runs the work, in the business, before building | `docs/planning/BOSS_INTERVIEW.md` — a capture sheet, filled in rounds, cross-referenced to open questions |
| The workflow was mapped from the business rather than imagined | `docs/planning/CURRENT_WORKFLOW.md`, `WORKFLOW_MAPS.md` |
| The first thing built was scoped by the business owner, not by the founder | `docs/planning/PROJECT_SCOPE.md` — the request-to-invoice pipeline is described as the boss's scope |
| The work was a replacement for a system the business was actually paying for and unhappy with | `docs/planning/CONTEXT.md` |
| Development continued past the original internal brief into a general architecture | `capability/`, `proof/`, `deployment/` |

The opening line of the interview sheet is worth quoting in the pitch, because it is the whole
story in one sentence and it was written before any of this existed:

> "I am mapping the current workflow before I build anything so I do not automate the wrong process."

That is a person inside a business being careful, not a student with an idea.

## What must be verified before it is said out loud

**Not written here, because nobody in this repository witnessed it.** These are the facts that
would make the story specific, and each needs Jack to confirm the exact wording. Fill them in here
once confirmed, with a date, and then never embellish them:

- The **role**: what Jack actually did at the business, in the words the business would use.
- The **period**: when, and for how long.
- **The specific incident.** Not "purchasing was inefficient" but one particular request, one
  particular day, and what it cost. A story needs one Tuesday, not a category.
- Whether the first system was built **while working there** or afterwards, and with whose
  knowledge. This matters and is easy to state ambiguously by accident.
- The relationship today: employee, contractor, former employee, family. **A judge may ask, and
  vagueness here is far more damaging than any answer.**

**Do not invent, round up, or dramatise any of these.** The founder story is the one part of the
pitch a judge cannot check — which is exactly why it must be the part that would survive checking.

---

## Structure

**Three beats, thirty seconds in the deck version, one clause in the four-minute version.**

1. **Proximity.** "I worked at an electrical contractor." One sentence. No résumé, no course, no
   ambitions.
2. **The specific moment.** The particular request that went wrong, or took a day, or got lost. It
   must be one incident with one person in it.
3. **What happened next.** Not "so I decided to found a company" — *"so I mapped the workflow before
   I built anything."* The credible version of this story is that the founder was careful, not that
   the founder was visionary.

Then stop. **The story earns the right to the rest of the pitch and does not compete with it.**

---

## Why you will keep building it

Asked in Q&A, not volunteered. The honest answer is the strongest one available and it is not about
passion:

> The question a customer asks in year two is not "did it run" but "what did it do for us", and
> almost nothing in this category can answer that. I built the answer to that question first,
> before the product had customers, because it is the part that is impossible to add later.

That is a statement about judgement and it is verifiable — `proof/` exists, is tested, and refuses
to produce figures it cannot defend. It is worth more than any statement about commitment.

## Prohibited

| Do not | Because |
|---|---|
| "I've always been an entrepreneur" | Unfalsifiable, and every other entry says it |
| Any claim about hours saved, in the founder story | Hours are a measured number and belong in the proof beat, where they trace to evidence |
| Framing the business owner as a victim of bad process | It is somebody's real company and they may hear about it. Describe the process, respect the people |
| A LinkedIn-shaped summary of skills and technologies | Not scored, and it converts a story into a CV |
| Implying sole authorship of things built with help | The one thing in the story a judge could check and disprove |
| Naming the company without permission | Ask first. `docs/design-partner/` treats this as a real question, and it is one |
